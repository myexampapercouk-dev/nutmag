An AI chatbot answers questions. An AI agent completes tasks: it checks your systems, makes decisions within your rules, takes actions like booking or updating records, and hands off to a person when it should. If you want fewer questions in your inbox, a chatbot may be enough. If you want work taken off your team’s plate, you need an agent.

## The short version

|  | AI chatbot | AI agent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main job | Answer questions | Get a task done |
| Knows | FAQs and pages you give it | Your live data: stock, orders, bookings, records |
| Can act? | Rarely | Yes: books, updates, sends, checks, routes |
| Follows business rules | Basic | Your pricing, limits, approvals and exceptions |
| When stuck | Says “please contact us” | Hands over to the right person with the full context |
| Typical result | Fewer repeat questions | Hours of manual work removed |

## The same customer, two different experiences

A customer messages a jewellery store: *“Do you have a gold necklace for a wedding, around my budget?”*

**With a chatbot:** it replies with a link to the necklaces page and suggests contacting the store. The customer scrolls, gets lost, and maybe leaves.

**With an AI agent:** it searches the store’s live catalogue by metal, style and price, shows three matching pieces with photos and current prices, and confirms they are in stock. When the customer says “I’d like to see this one”, it alerts the staff on WhatsApp with the chat summary.

Same question. One gives information; the other moves the sale forward.

## What makes something an agent

Three abilities separate an agent from a chatbot:

1. **Access to your systems.** It reads and updates the tools you already use — your store, CRM, calendar or accounting — through secure integrations.
2. **Decisions within your rules.** It knows your prices, policies and limits, and asks for approval when something falls outside them.
3. **Actions.** It does things: books a slot, creates a record, generates a document, sends a confirmation.

A well-built agent also logs every action and lets your team take over at any moment, so you stay in control.

## Which one does your business need?

Choose a **chatbot** if:

- Most questions are the same handful, with fixed answers
- Answers don’t depend on stock, bookings or customer records
- You want something you can set up yourself quickly

Choose an **AI agent** if:

- Answers depend on live data (“is it available?”, “where is my order?”)
- Your team copies information between systems by hand
- Leads or bookings slip through when you’re busy
- You want the work done, not just the question answered

## FAQ

**Is an AI agent harder to set up than a chatbot?** Yes, because it connects to your systems and follows your rules. That is also why it saves far more time. A good provider handles the setup for you.

**Can an AI agent make mistakes?** It can, which is why reliable agents work only from your data, ask for approval on anything sensitive, and are tested on real past cases before launch.

**Can I turn my existing chatbot into an agent?** Often, yes. The usual step is connecting it to one system first — for example your store or booking tool — and adding actions from there.

**Do agents replace staff?** In small businesses they mostly remove repetitive work, so your team spends time on customers and decisions instead of copying data and answering the same questions.
